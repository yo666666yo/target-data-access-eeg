\begin{abstract}
Cross-subject EEG decoders are compared across protocols that use the target subject differently, and the gaps between them are read as the value of that access---a reading that requires everything else to be matched. We divide each held-out subject's trials once into an available half and a reserved test half, score every condition on the same reserved half, and cross two procedures, Euclidean Alignment and supervised training on the available half, holding the source partition, the per-epoch sample budget, the target sampling rate and the scored trials fixed and verified against the realized indices. On three motor-imagery datasets spanning 9 to 105 subjects, and under one stated training policy, supervision helps on all three and alignment on two, but their ordering reverses---supervision leads alignment by $7.9$\,pp on BCI IV 2a and trails it by $4.2$\,pp on PhysionetMI---while no interaction replicates. Fixing the batch share does not fix exposure---each calibration trial is replayed $1.3$ to $16.6$ times per epoch depending on how many exist---but capping the calibration set within a dataset moves the effect by only $2$ of the $12$\,pp involved, so the reversal is not a budget artifact. A fixed-prediction control that only regroups identical predictions behaves oppositely: the between-subject spread exceeds what random groups of the same size give by $6.2$, $2.4$ and $1.8$ times, and a conditionally binomial correction attributes $2\%$, $17\%$ and $29\%$ of the observed variance to finite trials. That changes a reading of these data---raw spreads rank PhysionetMI as the more heterogeneous cohort, corrected ones do not---so a between-subject standard deviation needs the trials it came from.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Compliance with Ethical Standards}
This work is a secondary analysis of three public motor-imagery EEG corpora---BCI Competition IV
dataset 2a, BNCI2014-002 and the PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery database---each released by
its originators for open research use with participant consent and ethical approval obtained by
the original investigators. No new data were recorded and no human participants were involved in
this study, so no additional ethical approval was required. The authors declare no conflict of
interest.
